Kyzyl-Tas - a digital twin of an open-pit copper mine on OpenEgiz

Fictional open-pit Cu-Au mine with a 30 Mt/a concentrator, parametrised from public data for Kazakhstan's Bozshakol and Aktogay operations. Every mine object is an Eclipse Ditto twin; a physics-based engine drives them over MQTT; a 3D interface reads them back from the platform and runs what-if scenarios.

The mineA real-scale operation, checked against the reference site

  • Pit: 10 benches (waste cap above, ore below), main and alternate ore ramps, waste ramp, 12 haul-road segments.
  • Fleet: 14 haul trucks (12× Cat 785C 136 t, 2× Komatsu 830E-5 227 t with AC electric drive), 4 Hitachi EX3600 shovels, 2 drills.
  • Plant: gyratory crusher 4,186 t/h → 40 kt crushed-ore stockpile → SAG 28 MW + 2 ball mills 22 MW → flotation → tailings.
  • Stockpiles: ROM pad, crushed ore, high-grade reserve (grade control), waste dump.
  • Site: 214 MW substation, weather station (Kazakh steppe, down to −38 °C), 2 slope radars, dust stations.

Plant capacities, fleet types, power and climate: KAZ Minerals reports and presentations [S1-S5]; equipment specifications [S11-S14]. Full sourced parameter set: mine/docs/03_domain.md.

mine-ui (nginx + React/three.js) reads the twins from Ditto - REST for the catalogue, server-sent events for live changes - and history from InfluxDB. Its nginx adds the platform credentials server-side, so the browser needs no login.

What-if path: the interface writes PATCH .../things/kz.kyzyltas.mine:scenario → Ditto publishes the change on MQTT → the engine applies it → effects return through the equipment twins and the kpi twin.

Started by one command. deploy/compose/mine.yml adds mine-setup (creates the twins), mine-engine and mine-ui; the Makefile always loads it, so make up starts platform and mine together.

Platform fixes made for a clean Ubuntu VM: LF line endings, Telegraf topic filter and no per-command tags (series explosion), mine port in env/preflight/smoke, anonymous read-only Grafana, provisioned mine dashboard.

The first start downloads about 2.5 GB of images (several minutes); later starts take about a minute. make smoke checks the whole chain end to end.

Kyzyl-Tas · architecture3

Mine model21 type twins, 66 instance twins, one hierarchy

  • Types: each instance names its type twin in definition; type twins hold instance counts and the feature names and units.
  • Hierarchy in _parents: mine → pit → benches, radars; mine → dispatch → trucks, shovels, drills; mine → plant → crusher, conveyors, mills, flotation, tailings, stockpiles.
  • Material flow in feeds: shovels → ROM pad → crusher → stockpile → SAG → ball mills → flotation → tailings; the substation feeds the plant. Shovels name their bench.
  • Live relations: truck assigned_shovel and road_segment, road closed, grade-control reclaim_tph.
  • States: haul phase, health, queue, throughput, power, grade, drill rate, radar creep, PM10...
  • A contract test fails on any missing, extra or wrongly typed feature between engine and twins.

See it: Twin model button in the 3D view (no login), or Grafana → OpenEgiz → Twins / Types.

Energy, economicsdiesel by class/phase, grid power, CO₂ (0.80 kg/kWh), net smelter revenue

Interlinked. Trucks deliver ore at the grade of the face they loaded → ROM pad → crusher (plus high-grade ore when grade control asks) → 40 kt crushed-ore stockpile → SAG mill → flotation. Pit changes reach the plant only through the stockpiles - so haulage scenarios hit "ore mined" at once and plant output later.

Constraints and back-pressure: one truck under a shovel, two dump bays, winter speed caps, one ball mill down cuts grinding to 60 %, the crusher stops when the crushed pile is full and ore trucks wait when the ROM pad is full, closed ramp → 2.7 km detour, only electric trucks use the trolley line.

Shadow baseline: on every scenario click or Undo, a copy of the live mine (including random streams) continues without the scenario - the deltas measure that scenario only.

Kyzyl-Tas · behaviour5

What-if, step by stepShovel S2 failure - the causal chain on the stand

  1. shovel-s2 forced down (hydraulics, 10 mine hours) - red in 3D and on the minimap.
  2. Dispatch re-routes S2's trucks to S1 (event log: "rerouted").
  3. This screenshot, ~100 mine-minutes after the click: ore mined −30 %, ore grade 0.51 → 0.47 %, contained Cu mined −36 %, haul cycle +31 %. (Slide 7 gives two-hour averages, which include the first minutes.)
  4. Grade control reclaims the high-grade stockpile (800 t/h, highlighted).
  5. The crushed-ore buffer keeps the mill full: plant copper only −0.5 % so far; over 12 mine hours it falls 2.8 % on average (6.6 % with the fleet-management system offline).

Undo - back to baseline: every scenario field returns to its default, S2 returns to service at once, trucks are re-routed back, and the comparison restarts from zero.

Kyzyl-Tas · scenarios7

ModulesModules that decide while the mine runs - and energy accounting

Dispatch - Haulage and Dispatch

Sends each empty truck to the eligible shovel with the shortest queue; skips broken, closed-off or fume-blocked shovels. Offline, trucks of a broken shovel idle ~30 min until a radio reassignment. With dispatch and grade control, an S2 failure costs −23 % ore and −2.8 % copper; offline −28 % and −6.6 % (mean of 8 runs).

Grade control - Geology and Ore Quality

When ROM ore falls below 0.50 % Cu, reclaims the 0.65 % high-grade pile into the crusher feed in the proportion that restores the target (≤ 800 t/h). Seen in stockpile-hg.reclaim_tph and "grade control" events.

Predictive maintenance - Equipment Reliability

Health and hours to wear-out are estimated from coolant and oil-iron signals; work orders bring trucks in at the next shift change instead of failing in service. Over 200 mine hours (8 runs): unplanned failures −23 % (191 vs 249); availability about equal, since planned repairs also take trucks out.

Energy and CO₂ - Energy

Accounting, not a control decision: diesel by truck class and phase, grid power for crushing, grinding and trolley lines, cost and CO₂ at 0.80 kg/kWh. Trolley assist (two electric 830E trucks): diesel −4...−7 %, CO₂ about unchanged on the coal-heavy grid (break-even ≈ 0.64 kg/kWh).

Data and assumptionsReal sources, synthetic telemetry, stated limits

  • All telemetry is synthetic - generated by the engine. No company's operational data is used; no third-party dataset is stored.
  • The mine is fictional; its parameters come from public sources and carry tags: reported [R], vendor [V], literature [L], derived [D], assumed [A].
  • Sources: KAZ Minerals reports [S1-S5], equipment specs, Kuz-Ram, Bond, AP-42, Kazakh grid factors and tariffs, 2026 metal prices, reliability studies - listed in mine/docs/03_domain.md §12.
  • Assumptions [A]: road network and pit geometry, failure parameters, high-grade pile, grade-control target, grid factor.

Limitations

  • 14 trucks vs Bozshakol's 18: ore demand met, strip ratio ≈ 0.39 vs 0.7 planned.
  • Each haul leg timed as one block; per-segment traffic is estimated.
  • Price drop changes revenue only; the cut-off grade does not move.
  • Dispatch is a shortest-queue rule, not a full LP optimiser.
  • Wind, slope-radar velocity and fume delay exist only inside their presets; the other drivers are custom what-if sliders.

Verification: 58 engine tests (physics vs worked examples, mass balance, scenario directions, Undo, baseline copy, mill circuit, dispatch, twin/engine contract); tools/measure_scenarios.py and tools/validate.py reproduce every number here; make smoke checks the running stack.
